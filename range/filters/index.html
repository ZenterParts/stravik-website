What filters do

An engine burns thousands of litres of air for every litre of fuel, and every drop of oil and fuel passes through tight clearances. Filters keep dust, soot, metal particles and water out of the engine, the fuel system and the cabin.

Oil filter
Removes wear particles and soot from the engine oil. Where an anti-drain valve is fitted, it keeps oil in the filter so pressure builds quickly on a cold start.
Air filter
Stops dust and sand reaching the cylinders, where they wear the rings, liners and turbo.
Fuel filter
Removes dirt and, on diesel engines, water before it reaches the injection pump and injectors.
Cabin filter
Cleans the air coming into the cabin. Activated carbon versions also reduce odours and exhaust gases.
Coolant filter
Removes scale and debris from the cooling system on heavy-duty engines.

Signs a filter needs replacing

  • The service interval is reached (change the oil filter at every oil change)
  • Loss of power, poor throttle response or higher fuel use
  • Hard starting, misfiring or a water warning light on a diesel
  • A low oil pressure warning
  • Weak airflow, misted windows or odours in the cabin
  • The filter is dirty, damaged or soaked

Fitting tips

  • Change the oil filter every time you change the oil, and replace the sump plug washer.
  • Lightly oil the gasket of a spin-on filter, and tighten by hand to the torque printed on the filter.
  • Fit air and cabin filters with the airflow arrow the right way round, and clean the housing first.
  • After changing a diesel fuel filter, bleed the air out of the fuel system as the vehicle maker specifies.
  • Check for leaks after a short run.
