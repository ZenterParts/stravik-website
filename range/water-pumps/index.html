What a water pump does

The water pump keeps coolant moving between the engine, the radiator and the heater. Without that flow, engine heat builds up quickly, leading to overheating, a warped cylinder head or a failed head gasket.

Most pumps are driven by the engine through the timing belt or the auxiliary (V-ribbed) belt. Newer engines also use electric pumps, which a control unit switches on and off as needed. They can keep coolant moving after the engine stops and also cool the turbo, the EGR system and hybrid batteries.

Signs a water pump needs replacing

  • Coolant leaking from the pump's weep hole or the gasket face
  • Rising engine temperature or overheating
  • Whining or grinding from the bearing
  • Play or wobble in the pulley shaft
  • Low heater output
  • Warning light or fault code for an electric pump

Fitting tips

  • Replace the timing belt, tensioner and idlers when you replace a timing-belt-driven pump.
  • Flush the cooling system and fill with the coolant type the vehicle maker specifies.
  • Use only the gasket or sealant method the vehicle maker specifies.
  • Bleed the air out of the system after filling.
  • Check the bearing and seal after a short run.
